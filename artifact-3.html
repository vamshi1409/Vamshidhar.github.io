Title

Professor Predict: Understanding How Large Language Models Learn and Make Mistakes

Introduction

This artifact documents my Professor Predict AI Learning Activity for AIML-500: Machine Learning Fundamentals at Indiana Wesleyan University. It focuses on how large language models (LLMs) generate responses, why their answers can sometimes be incorrect, and how training data influences their performance. I organized this page for classmates, instructors, and professionals interested in evaluating AI-generated information responsibly.

Description

In this four-phase learning activity, I explored how large language models use learned patterns to produce responses. The exercises challenged me to recognize when I lacked enough information, examine factors that affect training data, and decide which tasks are appropriate for AI assistance. The activity also highlighted the difference between an answer that sounds convincing and one that can be verified.

Objective

To develop a clearer understanding of how LLMs generate text, identify the causes and risks of AI hallucinations, consider the effects of training data availability and representation, and evaluate when AI-generated responses need independent verification.

Process

  1. I completed the four phases of the Professor Predict learning activity and considered how LLMs produce answers.
  2. In the Velmora exercise, I recognized that I did not have sufficient information to provide a reliable answer rather than inventing details.
  3. I examined why a model may produce plausible but incorrect responses when familiar language patterns are mistaken for verified facts.
  4. I considered issues related to training data, including accessibility, licensing, privacy, and underrepresentation of less commonly represented languages.
  5. I evaluated different AI use cases. I identified creative writing as a suitable use and current stock prices as a case requiring live, verifiable data.
  6. I wrote a personal reflection explaining what I learned about the strengths, limitations, and responsible use of AI systems.

Tools and Technologies Used

Professor Predict course learning activity; written evaluation and reflection; and GitHub Pages for presenting the completed coursework in a professional portfolio. The activity focused on understanding LLM behavior rather than training or deploying a new model.

Key Learnings

  • Plausibility is not accuracy: an AI-generated answer can sound reasonable without being factually correct.
  • Training data matters: data availability and representation can affect how well an AI system responds across languages and topics.
  • Use cases differ: writing and explanation can benefit from generative AI, while current facts and high-stakes answers require additional checks.
  • Human judgment remains essential: users should recognize uncertainty and verify important claims.

Value Proposition

Unique Value. This artifact demonstrates my ability to question AI output rather than accept it automatically. I explain why hallucinations occur, identify limitations in training data, and recognize situations where additional evidence is needed.

Relevance. As a Full Stack .NET Developer, I work with software that must be dependable and testable. Understanding the limitations of AI-generated output helps me approach AI-powered features with greater attention to verification, user trust, and responsible design.

Personal Reflection

Before this activity, I knew that AI could make mistakes, but I did not fully understand why generated responses could sound correct even when they were wrong. The Velmora example reminded me to acknowledge when I do not have enough information. I also learned that differences in training data can affect the quality of answers across languages. Overall, this activity strengthened my habit of thinking critically and verifying AI-generated information when accuracy matters.
