Title

The Evolution of Artificial Intelligence: A Timeline from 1950 to 2026

Introduction

This artifact is a 7-slide digital timeline of artificial intelligence, from the Turing Test in 1950 to AI use in business in 2026. I created it for the AIML-500 Machine Learning Fundamentals course at Indiana Wesleyan University. It is written for hiring managers and teams in data and machine learning who want to see that I understand how AI developed and why progress came in waves.

The Timeline

Download the timeline (PDF)

Description

The first slide is an overview with six milestones: 1950 (questions), 1956 (AI is born), 1974 (first winter), 1986 (learning), 2012 (deep learning), 2022 (generative AI) and 2026 (adoption). Four slides cover four eras: the foundations and first AI winter (1950 to 1980), rules, setbacks and a comeback (1980 to 2006), the deep learning breakthrough (2009 to 2017) and generative AI going mainstream (2020 to 2026). Each has five dated milestones, such as the Turing Test, the Dartmouth workshop, the perceptron, ELIZA, backpropagation, Deep Blue, AWS EC2, ImageNet, AlexNet, GANs, AlphaGo, Transformers, GPT-3, ChatGPT and GPT-4. Each also has a note on why the era matters and its industry connection. Two closing slides list 19 sources and the AI assistance disclosure.

Objective

To show that I can research a broad topic and explain it clearly to a non-technical reader. The timeline argues that the turning point for AI was not a single invention: datasets, GPUs and better model designs advanced together, and each earlier boom was followed by a winter when promises outpaced results. It also notes that narrow capabilities, bias, privacy, reliability and energy costs still matter today.

Process

  1. I chose the key turning points in AI from 1950 to 2026 and grouped them into four eras.
  2. I researched each milestone and checked its date against credible historical and scholarly sources, listed on the last two slides.
  3. I designed the slides in PowerPoint with a milestone line for each era, a note on why it matters, and its industry connection.
  4. I wrote the AI assistance disclosure and exported the deck as a PDF for this portfolio.

Tools and Technologies Used

Microsoft PowerPoint for the editable slide design; ChatGPT for help with source research, date checking, event summaries and layout; published sources for the facts; GitHub Pages to publish this portfolio.

Value Proposition

Unique Value. The timeline does not only list events. Each era explains why it mattered and which industries it connects to, and it is clear about its scope: key turning points, not every discovery, and approximate dates for the AI winters.

Relevance. Teams that build or use AI need people who understand why earlier systems failed and what changed, so they can set realistic expectations for new tools.
