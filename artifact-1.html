Title

DriveMate AI: A Car-Buying Chatbot Built and Tested in Mizou

Introduction

This artifact is the documentation for DriveMate AI, an educational chatbot I built in Mizou for the AIML-500 Machine Learning Fundamentals course (AI Lab 1.4). It is written for university students and first-time car buyers, and for hiring managers who want to see how I design, test and critically evaluate an AI product.

Download the full documentation (PDF)

Description

The document covers the problem, learning objectives and a design thinking approach (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test); a ten-question bot-building plan; a comparison of three ChatGPT replies to one prompt; a six-turn custom GPT follow-up exercise; a research review of the STORM tool; the Mizou configuration; seven live tests of the published bot with screenshots; and a section on ethics, privacy and reliability. It states clearly what each piece of evidence does and does not show.

Objective

To help a first-time buyer compare vehicles, understand financing basics such as down payment, APR and loan term, and know what to verify with a real dealer or lender, without the bot inventing prices, promising loans or confirming appointments. In live testing, DriveMate AI explained financing, declined to guarantee 0% interest and declined to book a test drive. Two weaknesses were found: vehicle details that I could not confirm against my prepared sample file, and a changed budget that did not carry over the earlier SUV preference.

Process

  1. I defined the problem and success criteria for first-time buyers, then answered the ten planning questions for the bot.
  2. I ran one prompt in three separate ChatGPT chats and compared accuracy, clarity and instruction following, then ran a six-turn follow-up exercise with a custom GPT.
  3. I configured DriveMate AI in Mizou: title, AI name, learning objective, welcome message and a rule limiting replies to 500 characters.
  4. I tested the live bot with seven buyer questions and recorded each reply with screenshots.
  5. I tested a separate scripted local prototype, found a budget-change bug (7 of 8 checks passed), fixed it and retested (8 of 8). I kept these results separate from the live Mizou results.
  6. I wrote a proposed rule improvement for the live bot and listed the evidence still missing.

Tools and Technologies Used

Mizou (chatbot platform); ChatGPT (comparison chats and custom GPT exercise); a scripted HTML prototype tested in a headless Chromium browser; the Stanford STORM project documentation; and public guidance from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the Federal Trade Commission and NHTSA. GitHub Pages hosts this portfolio.

Value Proposition

Unique Value. I test AI output against evidence instead of trusting it. This project separates live results from prototype results, flags facts I could not verify, and turns each weakness into a specific rule improvement.

Relevance. Teams that deploy chatbots need people who can set limits on what a bot may claim, test it with real user questions and report honestly where it fails.
